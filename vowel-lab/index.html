How to read the map

Each patch encloses the central readings from one excerpt where every sounding part shares a syllable. Separate patches preserve differences between examples. A patch measures the recorded mix. Its size can reflect changes in the vowel, the room or the estimate.

Medial vowel frames were checked with two Praat Burg settings and VoiceSu’s classical LPC tracker. Up to three examples per vowel favor agreement, stability and coverage. Some glides yield only an early or late reading. An arrow connects two windows when their centers move beyond the observed scatter.

Solid keys have two measured phases or a steady vowel. A half-filled key has only partial glide evidence. An open key has no measured region. Split underlines mark alternative pronunciations. Hums have no vowel target.

Dashed patches name the other song when it supplies an example. Crosses mark published speech averages where available. English R-coloring can also depend on F3.

Chart settings

The diamond plays the two resonances under your cursor at your last sung pitch. Before your first note it uses A3. With the chart focused, use arrow keys to move and hold Space to listen.

The microphone dot shows F1 and F2 in Hz, estimated by linear prediction from the shape of your spectrum. At high pitches, widely spaced harmonics make the resonances harder to locate. A hollow dot marks these estimates.
